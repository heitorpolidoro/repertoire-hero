Answered — drag wins, with a handle

You asked whether a press on the dots could drag while a press anywhere else still scrolls. Yes, and it is the normal way to build this: the browser decides by where the finger landed, not by how far it moved, so touch-action: none on the handle alone is the whole mechanism. The old argument — "a drag has to first prove it is not a scroll" — only ever applied to dragging the whole row, and is withdrawn. Drag is adopted. The up/down arrows stay as the keyboard and screen-reader path.

3. What ships: a reorder mode

One Reorder toggle in the playlist header. While it is on, each row drops the duration, the four-note control, the remove button and the tag row — none of them is what you are doing — and spends that space on a 44 px grip plus the 44 px up/down pair. Title/artist gets 126 px, more than the 82 px it has the rest of the time. While it is off, the row is exactly what RH-102 leaves behind. Nothing yields permanently.

    Anatomy of the reorder-mode row

    Grip, 44 px wide × full row height. The only element with touch-action:none. Six dots, leading edge, where a thumb lands. Target is ~44 × 56 px, above the 44 px minimum on both axes.

    Up / down pair, 44 × 44 each. Unchanged names Move <title> up / down, disabled at the ends. This is the keyboard and screen-reader path; it is not optional and does not depend on the drag.

    Hidden while reordering: duration, the four-note status control, the remove button, the tag row. All back the moment you press Done.

    Insertion line. The dragged row lifts and follows the finger; a 2 px emerald line marks where it will land. The other rows do not move, so the list never jumps under you.

    Cancel. Esc, or a cancelled pointer (a call, a system gesture), puts the row back and writes nothing.
